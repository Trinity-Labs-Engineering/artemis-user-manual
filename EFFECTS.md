# Digital Effects Reference

**ArtemisOS / Houston v1.0.0**

[Return to the user manual](README.md#digital-effects).

Open a virtual block, choose an effect from the drawer and open its focus view to edit it. This reference covers the pedal choices exposed by the editor, followed by the amp and cab stages. Imported captures add their own entries to your library.

The starting points below are suggestions to try by ear, not factory presets. Begin at a low listening level, compare with bypass, and adjust the effect's output to avoid a loudness jump. “Low” and “mid” refer to the control's displayed range. Use the units shown on screen; similarly named controls can have different scales.

Save the preset after editing. For expression ranges and tempo sources, see [expression assignment](README.md#expression-calibration-and-assignment) and [MIDI clock](README.md#clock-synchronisation-and-midi-thru).

## Boost

Boosts can raise level, change tonal emphasis or push a following drive harder. Put a boost before a drive for more saturation; try it after the drive when you primarily want more volume.

| Effect | Controls | Starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gain | Gain | Use a small increase for a solo lift, then check headroom in the following stage. |
| Linear Power Booster | Boost | Start low and increase until the next drive responds more strongly. |
| Vintage Bass Booster I | Volume, Mix | Try a low Mix for added low-frequency weight, then match Volume to bypass. |
| Vintage Bass Booster II | Boost, Mix | Add a little Mix and raise Boost gradually for a fuller sound. |
| High Freq Booster I | Level | Start low for extra brightness ahead of a dark drive sound. |
| High Freq Booster II | Intensity, Volume | Keep Intensity low initially, then balance Volume after choosing the emphasis. |
| Rangefinder | Boost, Mix | Try a moderate Boost and restrained Mix into an overdriven amp stage. |

## Compression and gating

Dynamics effects respond to playing level. Adjust them while playing both quiet and strong notes. Threshold depends on the signal feeding the effect, so a setting that works with one guitar may need changing for another.

| Effect | Purpose and controls | Starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compressor | Reduces level differences. Attack, Knee, Ratio, Release and Threshold determine when and how strongly it acts. | Use a gentle Ratio, then lower Threshold until strong notes are controlled without losing all their attack. |
| Expander | Increases the difference between quiet and loud signals. Attack, Knee, Ratio, Release and Threshold control the response. | Set Threshold just above the unwanted low-level sound and use a gentle Ratio to retain natural note endings. |
| Noise Gate | Attenuates the signal below Threshold. Attack, Hold and Decay shape opening and closing; Range sets attenuation. | Raise Threshold until idle noise is reduced, then lengthen Hold or Decay if sustained notes end abruptly. |

## Delay

BPM controls the repeat rate for these effects. Where offered, select it to choose a manual tempo, expression source or MIDI Clock and subdivision. Higher feedback can build into a loud sustained sound; raise it gradually.

| Effect | Purpose and controls | Starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delay | Simple tempo-based delay with BPM and Gain. | Set a tempo that fits the song and keep Gain low for an understated repeat. |
| Digital Delay | Repeats with BPM, Feedback, Highpass, Lowpass and Level. | Use low Feedback and Level, then narrow the repeat's frequency range with the filters so it sits behind the dry notes. |
| Duck Delay | Delay that responds to playing dynamics. Attack, Release and Amount control the ducking response; BPM and Feedback shape the repeats. | Start with low Feedback, then adjust Amount so repeats sit back while you play and emerge between phrases. |
| Echo | Tempo-based echo with BPM and Percent. | Start with a low Percent and adjust while playing short phrases to hear the echo clearly. |
| Reverse Delay | Reversed delay texture with Mix, Feedback, BPM and Window. | Use low Feedback and a modest Mix. Adjust Window while listening for the length and character of the reversed fragments. |

| B-Fuzz | Drive, Level, Thickness, Mix | Keep Thickness and Mix low while choosing the Drive amount. |
| Fuzz Freak | Fuzz, Gate, Level, Tone, Mix | Start with low Gate so notes sustain; add Gate for a more abrupt ending. |
| Foxey Lady | Fuzz, Level | Choose moderate Fuzz and match Level to bypass. |
| Fuzz Master | Level, Tone, Mix | Start with low Mix and central Tone, then increase the blend. |
| Fuzz Drive | Distortion, Level, Mix | Use a low Distortion setting and modest Mix for a blended texture. |

| Fuzz-FM | Drive, Fuzz, Input, Level | Keep Input and Drive restrained while exploring Fuzz, then balance Level. |

## EQ and filters

Use EQ to make a sound fit the instrument and listening system. Cutting a troublesome band often leaves more headroom than boosting other bands around it.

| Effect | Purpose and controls | Starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low/High Pass Filter | Frequency filtering with two High Freq and two Low Freq controls in separate positions. | Adjust one cutoff at a time while listening for the part of the spectrum it removes. Keep other controls unchanged until its action is clear. |
| Baxandall | Broad Bass and Treble shaping. | Begin near the middle of each displayed range; adjust one control at a time. |
| Bi-Quad Filter | Frequency-focused filtering with Freq. | Sweep Freq slowly while playing to find a useful emphasis or cut. |
| 4-Band EQ | Four level bands with individual Bandwidth controls. The displayed level labels include Low Cut, Level 2, Level 3 and High Cut. | Start the level bands at neutral, make a small cut in one band and adjust its width. |
| 11-Band Graphic EQ | Frequency-band levels from the low end through 16k, with Low Cut and High Cut labels at the ends. | Start flat and make a small cut in the band nearest an unwanted resonance. |
| 11-Band Parametric EQ | Band levels plus Q controls that adjust band width/selectivity. | Start with a small level adjustment and a broad band; narrow it only when targeting a specific resonance. |

Some labels repeat or abbreviate the affected band. Use the control's position and displayed value to distinguish it, and compare with bypass after each change.

## Pitch

Pitch effects work best when the input notes are clear. Test tracking with the instrument, register and playing style you will use.

| Effect | Purpose and controls | Starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pitch Shift | One shifted voice with Detune, Dry and Wet. | Use a small Detune and a low Wet level alongside Dry for a subtle doubled sound. |
| Dual Pitch | Two voices with Voice 1, Voice 2, V1 Level, V2 Level and Dry. | Choose small opposing shifts and keep both voice levels below Dry. |
| Harmoniser | A scale-aware voice using Tonic, Mode, Interval, Wet and Dry. | Set the song's tonic and mode first, choose the desired interval and keep Wet modest while testing the melody. |
| Dual Harmoniser | Two scale-aware intervals with Tonic, Mode, Voice 1 Interval, Voice 2 Interval, Voice Mix and Dry. | Set the key/mode, choose the two intervals and blend the voices beneath Dry. |

An incorrect tonic or mode can create unwanted harmony notes even when tracking is working. Check the song's key changes before using the same harmony throughout.

## Modulation

BPM sets the rate of tempo-based modulation. Depth and Mix determine how prominent the movement is. Where the control offers Clock assignment, save that assignment with the preset to follow an external tempo.

| Effect | Purpose and controls | Starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mono Chorus | Chorus with BPM, Level and Mix. | Use a slow rate and low Mix, then balance Level for gentle movement. |
| Mono Flanger | Sweeping flanger with Depth, BPM and Mix. | Try a slow rate and shallow Depth before increasing Mix. |
| Mono Flanger Plus | Flanging with Depth, Feedback, BPM, Mix, Wet and Width. | Begin with low Feedback, moderate Width and a restrained wet blend. |
| Mono Phaser | Phase sweep with Depth, BPM and Mix. | Use a slow rate and moderate Depth for a gradual sweep. |
| Ring Modulator | Frequency multiplication texture with Mix and Freq. | Keep Mix low while sweeping Freq to find a useful metallic texture. |
| Tremolo | Level modulation with Depth, BPM and Mix. | Set a song-related rate, then raise Depth until the pulse is clear without fully cutting the notes. |
| Mono Vintage Vibe | Modulation with Depth, Feedback, BPM, Mix and Width. | Start with slow BPM, low Feedback and a modest Mix. |

The virtual blocks are mono. An effect's Width control does not turn the block into a stereo send/return path; use the final physical stereo loop for a stereo pedal.

| Effect | Controls | Starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TLA95 | Volume, Hotpotz | Assign Hotpotz to expression, then narrow its range if either end is too extreme. |
| Classic wah | Level, Wah, Mix | Assign Wah to expression and begin with a moderate Mix. |

## Reverb

**Freeverb** provides **Size**, **Damp** and **Mix**. Size changes the space/decay character, Damp shapes the high-frequency decay, and Mix blends the result with the dry signal.

For a subtle space, begin with low Size and Mix, then raise Damp if the tail is too bright. For a larger sustained texture, increase Size gradually and check that the reverb does not mask the next phrase. Test preset transitions before relying on tails during performance.

## Neural captures

**NAM** in the Neural pedal category uses a capture with input and output level controls. In the amp stage, the equivalent controls appear as **Input Gain** and **Output Gain**.

Start with the capture's initial levels, compare with bypass, and adjust output to balance the sound. Changing input gain changes the level driving the captured model and can change its saturation. It is not equivalent to adjusting an individual tone or gain knob on the original captured hardware.

A downloaded collection may offer different captures or variants. Choose the appropriate one, then save the preset. Keep the referenced file in the library. Follow [TONE3000 downloads](README.md#downloading-nams-from-tone3000) or [USB import](README.md#importing-nams-and-irs-over-usb) to add captures.

## Amp and cab stages

The amp and cab positions follow the digital pedal chain inside each virtual block.

For a built-in amp, select a model from **Amps**, open its focus view and adjust the controls offered by that model. Start with restrained gain and broadly central tone settings, then balance its output. Different models expose different controls; selecting a named model is a starting point for your sound.

For a captured amp, use **Amps → NAM**. Decide whether the capture includes a cabinet: an amp-plus-cab capture often works without another cab stage, while an amp-only capture is commonly paired with a cab response for a full-range output.

Use **Cabs** for the cabinet stage and select the required model or imported IR. Compare the complete chain at its intended output:

- Into a full-range monitor or recording path, try an amp and cab together.
- Into a guitar amplifier and physical guitar cabinet, compare with the cab stage bypassed.
- For a captured pedal ahead of an amp, place the NAM in a pedal position under **Pedals → Neural**.

These are starting arrangements, not fixed rules. Avoid adding a second cabinet response accidentally, check the final level and save the preset once the complete route works.
